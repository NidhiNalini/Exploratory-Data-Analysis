# Understanding the Problem

## Purpose

Redwood Delivery Co. wanted to better understand routine delivery-stop delays before reporting performance metrics or making service communication decisions. The goal of this analysis was to examine the distribution of delivery-stop delays, identify any unusual records that may affect reporting, and provide evidence-based observations using a univariate exploratory approach.

The central business question was:

> What does the distribution of delivery-stop delay tell the dispatch manager, and which records need review before routine performance is reported?

Because this is an exploratory observational dataset, the analysis focuses on describing patterns and identifying unusual observations rather than determining causes.

---

## Dataset Description

The dataset contained 22 fictional delivery-stop records. Each row represented one completed delivery stop. The dataset included the following variables:

- `delivery_id`: unique identifier for each delivery stop
- `delivery_window`: morning, afternoon, or evening service period
- `route_type`: urban, suburban, or rural route classification
- `delay_minutes`: number of minutes late compared with the planned delivery time
- `package_weight_kg`: total package weight delivered at the stop

Before beginning the analysis, the dataset was validated to ensure that:

- all required columns were present;
- every delivery record contained an identifier;
- all delivery identifiers were unique.

The validation checks passed successfully.

---

## Guided Analysis: Delivery Delay

### Data Preparation

The `delay_minutes` variable was converted to a numeric data type to ensure accurate calculations and visualizations.

During data quality checks, one missing delay value was identified. As a result, 21 delivery records were available for delay analysis.

### Summary Statistics

The following summary statistics were calculated for delivery delay:

| Measure | Value |
|----------|---------|
| Usable Records | 21 |
| Mean Delay | 32.90 minutes |
| Median Delay | 22.0 minutes |
| Minimum Delay | 3 minutes |
| Maximum Delay | 140 minutes |
| First Quartile (Q1) | 12 minutes |
| Third Quartile (Q3) | 39 minutes |
| Interquartile Range (IQR) | 27 minutes |

### Visual Exploration

A histogram and box plot were created to visualize the distribution of delivery delays.

The histogram showed that most delivery delays were clustered at the lower end of the distribution. Many delivery stops experienced delays between approximately 3 and 40 minutes.

The box plot revealed several unusually large delay values that were substantially higher than the majority of observations.

### Outlier Review

An Interquartile Range (IQR) method was used to identify unusually high delay observations.

The calculated upper fence identified two records requiring review:

| Delivery ID | Delay (minutes) |
|-------------|----------------|
| DL-020 | 95 |
| DL-021 | 140 |

These records were saved in `possible_high_delays.csv`.

These observations should not be removed from the dataset because they are valid recorded values. However, they should be reviewed before routine performance summaries are reported because they may significantly influence average delay measurements.

---

## Findings and Interpretation

The average delivery delay was approximately 32.9 minutes, while the median delay was 22 minutes.

The fact that the mean is considerably higher than the median suggests that the distribution is positively skewed. In other words, a small number of unusually large delays are pulling the average upward.

The histogram supports this conclusion by showing that most delivery stops experienced relatively modest delays, while only a few deliveries experienced extremely large delays. The box plot further confirmed the presence of these unusually high values.

From a business perspective, the dispatch manager should recognize that the average delay alone may not accurately represent typical performance. The median delay of 22 minutes provides a better indication of a typical delivery-stop delay because it is less affected by extreme observations.

Before performance results are communicated, the two flagged delivery records should be reviewed to determine whether they represent exceptional operational events, data-entry issues, or legitimate service delays.

## Independent Analysis: Package Weight

### Purpose

For the independent univariate analysis, `package_weight_kg` was selected because it is a numerical variable suitable for statistical summaries and graphical exploration.

### Summary Statistics

The analysis produced the following results:

| Measure | Value |
|----------|---------|
| Records | 22 |
| Mean Weight | 12.55 kg |
| Median Weight | 10.85 kg |
| Minimum Weight | 4.8 kg |
| Maximum Weight | 27.4 kg |

### Visualization

A histogram was created to examine the distribution of package weights.

The histogram showed that most deliveries involved lighter packages, with a concentration of observations below approximately 16 kilograms. Only a small number of delivery stops involved heavier package weights.

### Findings

The average package weight was approximately 12.55 kilograms, while the median package weight was approximately 10.85 kilograms. Similar to the delay analysis, the mean is slightly higher than the median, indicating that a small number of heavier deliveries increase the overall average.

Most delivery stops appear to involve lightweight or moderately weighted shipments, while heavy deliveries are less common.

### Recommendation

A practical next step would be to continue monitoring package-weight distributions across future delivery periods. This would help identify unusual shipment patterns, changes in customer demand, or operational planning requirements.

---

## Limitations

This analysis was strictly univariate, meaning each variable was examined independently. While the analysis successfully described the distribution of delivery delays and package weights, it cannot determine why delays occurred or whether any relationship exists between package weight, route type, delivery window, and delay outcomes.

No causal conclusions can be drawn from this analysis. Additional multivariate analysis would be required to investigate potential relationships among variables.

---

## Conclusion

The delivery delay distribution was positively skewed, with most deliveries experiencing relatively modest delays and a small number of unusually large delays increasing the average. The median delay of 22 minutes provides a more representative measure of typical performance than the mean delay of 32.9 minutes.

Two records, DL-020 and DL-021, were identified as potential high-delay observations and should be reviewed before routine performance reporting.

The independent analysis showed that most package weights were relatively low, with only a small number of heavier deliveries increasing the overall average package weight.

Overall, the analysis provides a useful baseline description of current delivery-stop performance while highlighting records that deserve additional review.
